# Voice: Spiraling Complicity

The voice spirals outward (that's the structural core), and the narrator is always complicit — never observing from outside, always implicated in the failure or absurdity. The two things move together.

## Who the narrator is

First-person, always implicated. The narrator is never just an observer — they are the problem, the failure, the one escalating. They are self-aware but this self-awareness changes nothing. They perform intelligence to avoid emotional honesty. The reader sees through this; the narrator sometimes doesn't.

---

## Sentence rhythm

Write as if someone is thinking out loud, not polishing prose.

Sentences vary wildly in length. Short declarations can land next to long baroque constructions. The syncopation is intentional.

Use parenthetical asides to show thought branching mid-sentence. 

---

## Escalation pattern

Start mundane. Spiral outward in concentric circles — logically, but into increasing absurdity. The escalation doesn't climax; it mutates.

- Dating problem → poisoned dog → utilitarian philosophy (*Boiled Tomato Technique*)
- Spontaneous travel → no shelter → frozen all night → mocking Lao Tzu (*Bench It Is*)
- Cucumber ice cream request → epistemology → ice cream cone stabbing (*Flavour of Facts*)

The reader expects resolution. Give them deflation, a tonal shift, or a joke that undermines everything.

---

## How pieces open

- Arresting first-person admission: *"Shit, I can no longer have a decent wank."*
- Vivid concrete image treated matter-of-factly: *"On the beach you'll see people walking across the beach."*
- Dialogue without context, in medias res.
- Formal/clinical exposition that turns out to be absurd.

## How pieces close

- Abrupt deflation: *"I thought about adopting that kid, but then again I don't need a chauffeur."*
- Anticlimactic return to mundanity after spectacular failure.
- Final punchline that retroactively undermines the whole piece: *"I was driving the tractor."*
- Unanswered question that becomes the answer.
- Never: a lesson learned, a character changed, a relationship healed.

---

## Dialogue

- Minimal stage directions. Dialogue carries all weight.
- Repetition used for comic dissociation: characters repeat themselves until words lose meaning.
- Silence and ellipsis are dialogue: `"Er.." / "..." / "..."`
- Characters say what they're supposed to say, then contradict themselves.
- Dialogue exposes social performance, not authentic communication.
- No adverbs on dialogue tags. Never *"she said warmly."* Just *"she said,"* or nothing.

---

## Humour mechanics

- **Literalise the metaphor.** Take abstract claims and make them concretely, absurdly literal.
- **Commit to the absurd.** Batman on the street, whistling — treated matter-of-factly.
- **Intellectual overreach.** Use academic register to justify trivial or cruel acts.
- **Tonal whiplash.** Profound → crude. Philosophical → sexual. No warning.
- **Never explain the joke.** Trust the reader. The joke lands or it doesn't.
- **Repetition until dissociation.** Repeat a phrase until it becomes strange.

---

## Philosophical tangents

Introduced via: quotation hook, personal experience trigger, or as obstruction to avoid emotional truth.

Resolved by: abandonment, contradiction by the next action, or revealed as self-serving rationalization.

Philosophy is never presented as *true*. It is the narrator's desperate attempt to impose meaning on meaningless things.

---

## Vocabulary and register

Moves freely between: crude/explicit ("wank," "shit"), technical/professional ("logistical failure," "key performance indicators"), baroque ("revolting charm of despotism"), colloquial ("that's cool man, dude"), and invented terms ("Tropelero").

Inflated vocabulary appears around absurd subjects for comic effect.

Spanish phrases woven in without translation: *"qué pasa, niño," "cariño," "sols el poble salva el poble."* Don't translate them unless the piece requires it.

---

## Format patterns

When writing **dialogue-heavy pieces**: minimal stage directions, let dialogue carry confusion and contradiction, break the format when the content calls for it.

When writing **timestamp/journey format**: precise times create false precision. Document failure with documentary seriousness.

When writing **list structures**: lists create a false sense of order that gets undermined. Repetition shows how systems pretend to function.

All formats: form should mirror content. Chaos in structure reflects chaos in substance.
