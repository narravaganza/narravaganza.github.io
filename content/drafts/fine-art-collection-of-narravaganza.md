<div class="collection-introduction">
  <p>The Narravaganza Collection of Art That's Fine brings together sixteen works whose importance has, until now, been protected by their almost total absence from public discussion. Presented here in a sequence determined by wall availability, they trace the movement of the artist’s name from cultural inheritance to practical instruction.</p>
  <p>The Collection invites sustained looking. Visitors wishing to sustain it further may scroll.</p>
</div>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape collection-frame--procedural-gilt collection-frame--procedural-landscape"><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--top" aria-hidden="true"></span><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--right" aria-hidden="true"></span><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--bottom" aria-hidden="true"></span><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--left" aria-hidden="true"></span><img src="/assets/images/artists/vincent_van_gogh.png" alt="Vincent van Gogh driving a yellow postal van through a swirling Provençal landscape, with a sunflower painting on its roof."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Vincent Van Gogh</strong>
    <span><em>The Postal Horizon</em>, 1888</span>
    <span>Sunflower seed oil on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The van makes movement visible while withholding any certainty that arrival will follow. A second painting, secured to the roof, converts the vehicle into both transport and exhibition architecture. Authorship is therefore displaced from the studio into a logistical field governed by routes, signatures and the availability of somebody at home between nine and one.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/edgar_degastation.png" alt="Edgar Degas holding a petrol nozzle beside ballet dancers at a filling station."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Edgar Degastation</strong>
    <span><em>Rehearsal at Pump Three</em>, 1909</span>
    <span>Oil on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The nozzle extends the artist’s hand into an economy of fuel, discipline and repeated movement. Around him, dancers rehearse gestures whose apparent effortlessness depends upon forms of energy supplied elsewhere. Whether Degas is serving them or merely waiting for them to finish stretching remains unresolved. The station has asked that visitors refrain from resolving it near the pumps.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--square"><img src="/assets/images/artists/candinsky.png" alt="A tin can surrounded by angular planes, circles and intersecting lines in yellow, blue, red and black."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Candinsky</strong>
    <span><em>Composition with Undisclosed Contents</em>, 1926</span>
    <span>Oil, tinplate and canning enamel on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The can occupies the composition as a volume that is materially present yet epistemically sealed. Lines approach it, touch its perimeter and depart without gaining access. Earlier scholarship identified peas; subsequent work proposed beans. Opening the can would settle the question while destroying the interpretive community that has formed around it, including an annual symposium with a very good lunch.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--portrait"><img src="/assets/images/artists/mondrianna.png" alt="Rihanna's portrait intersected by a Mondrian-style black grid with blocks of red, yellow, blue and white."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Pete Mondrianna</strong>
    <span><em>Study for an Absent Umbrella</em>, 1930</span>
    <span>Oil and cosmetic pigment on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The grid promises to release painting from the obligation to resemble anyone. Rihanna remains recognisable, occupying several rectangles without appearing to have booked them. Red, yellow and blue establish a universal language whose universality now depends upon knowing who she is. The Collection’s request for a reproduction licence has been forwarded to a record label.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--portrait"><img src="/assets/images/artists/francis_bacon_sandwich.png" alt="A screaming Francis Bacon enclosed between slices of bread and strips of bacon, beside a sixpence price card."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Francis Bacon Sandwich</strong>
    <span><em>Sandwich</em>, 1953</span>
    <span>Oil and rendered animal fat on greaseproof paper</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">Framing becomes compression as the body is reorganised between bread. The surrounding bacon denies any stable distinction between portrait and filling, while the price card introduces value without clarifying what the sixpence includes. The scream may register bodily exposure, existential refusal or an objection to the absence of brown sauce. These readings need not be mutually exclusive.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--portrait collection-frame--procedural-gilt collection-frame--procedural-portrait"><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--top" aria-hidden="true"></span><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--right" aria-hidden="true"></span><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--bottom" aria-hidden="true"></span><span class="collection-frame-carving collection-frame-carving--left" aria-hidden="true"></span><img src="/assets/images/artists/frida_kahlories.png" alt="A monumental Frida Kahlo sits among flowers beneath personified sun and moon, with a black bird at her shoulder."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Frida Kahlories</strong>
    <span><em>Self-Portrait with Recommended Daily Allowance</em>, 1941</span>
    <span>Oil and fortified cereal dust on board</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">Kahlo’s expanded body refuses the portrait’s traditional promise that identity can be contained by an edge. Flowers multiply across dress, landscape and crown until decoration becomes an ecology of appetite. Sun and moon observe without intervening, positioning consumption within a cosmic rhythm of intake and expenditure. The bird appears to be withholding a personal remark.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/paul_kleenex.png" alt="Paul Klee emerging among white tissues from a patterned box, against a landscape of abstract shapes."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Paul Kleenex</strong>
    <span><em>Portrait with Lotion</em>, 1931</span>
    <span>Mixed media on paper</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The face appears at the point where tissue leaves the box, locating individual emergence within a system designed to produce the next individual immediately. Softness functions here as both care and planned disposability. An acid-free enclosure was proposed, then withdrawn when it became clear that a box around the box would generate administrative implications extending beyond the current conservation budget.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--portrait"><img src="/assets/images/artists/mc_escher.png" alt="M. C. Escher holding a microphone amid impossible staircases, tessellated birds, a DJ and crowds facing conflicting directions."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>MC Escher</strong>
    <span><em>Relativity (Extended Mix)</em>, 1962</span>
    <span>Lithograph, club stamp and bass residue on paper</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The microphone proposes a single centre of address while the architecture distributes its audience across incompatible planes. Each crowd may understand itself to be facing the performer; no view can confirm these claims together. A reconstruction was suspended after the fire inspector’s route of egress returned him to the cloakroom twice, where he was charged for a second coat.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/gericault_of_duty.png" alt="Shipwrecked men gaming on a raft, with monitors among the bodies and one survivor raising a console overhead."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Géricault of Duty</strong>
    <span><em>Modern Raftfare</em>, 1819</span>
    <span>Mixed media on raft timber with four-player adapter</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The raised console redirects the composition’s appeal towards a horizon understood primarily as signal. Below it, bodies occupy privately negotiated fields of action within a shared catastrophe. The sea threatens everyone, although several figures are waiting for a match to load. Technical questions concerning the power supply remain secondary to the work’s larger enquiry into why nobody brought a spare controller.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/hieronymus_bosch.png" alt="A triptych of tormented figures among towers of washing machines, laundry baskets and monstrous attendants."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Hieronymus Bosch</strong>
    <span><em>The Garden of Detergent Delights</em>, c. 1510</span>
    <span>Oil and laundry starch on panel</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">Purification produces an expanding architecture of maintenance. Every machine offers completion; every basket postpones it into another load. People carry laundry, enter appliances and emerge as work for somebody else. A creature irons the garment of an absent owner, condemned to labour without encountering the finished pile. The Collection has asked whether it might also do curtains.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/max_bill.png" alt="A one trillion franc banknote designed in Swiss modernist style, wrapped in a spiral around the Matterhorn."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Max Bill</strong>
    <span><em>Untitled (Invoice)</em>, 1971</span>
    <span>Offset print on paper, 1:1 scale</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">A denomination not yet required by the Swiss National Bank wraps the Matterhorn in a continuous gesture, binding together two national reserves. The mountain supplies scale; the note supplies valuation. Too large to display and too valuable to insure, the original is represented by its certificate of authenticity. The certificate is represented by this image. Change is not available.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--square"><img src="/assets/images/artists/michael_jackson_pollock.png" alt="A fish in a black fedora emerges from a dense field of black, white, gold, red and blue paint splashes."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Michael Jackson Pollock</strong>
    <span><em>The Fish Is Not My Son</em>, 1950</span>
    <span>Toy slime, glitter glue and wax crayons on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The fish emerges from the all-over field not as a figure placed upon abstraction, but as abstraction’s own delayed recognition of its body. Hat and gesture introduce performance into the horizontal logic of the pour. Conservation analysis found no sequins, though their absence now operates as a particularly restrained form of sparkle. The work should not be rotated, even briefly.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--portrait"><img src="/assets/images/artists/michelangelo_david_bowie.png" alt="Michelangelo's David reimagined with David Bowie's hair and red-and-blue lightning bolt makeup."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Michelangelo Buonarroti</strong>
    <span><em>David Bowie</em>, 1504–1973</span>
    <span>Injection-moulded PVC and theatrical makeup</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">Renaissance permanence encounters the modern demand for reinvention. The lightning bolt does not conceal the marble surface; it activates the face as a site through which identity may arrive theatrically and depart before becoming administrative. The raised hand appears poised between sling, microphone and the discovery that the garment supplied by wardrobe has no visible opening.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--square"><img src="/assets/images/artists/pablo_pikachu.png" alt="A cubist portrait combining Pablo Picasso's face with the yellow features and red cheek of Pikachu."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Pablo Pikachu</strong>
    <span><em>Portrait of the Artist, Fully Charged</em>, 1937</span>
    <span>Oil and copper foil on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">Multiple viewpoints organise the face around an energy that cannot be contained by anatomical continuity. The red cheek functions as reservoir, wound and compositional counterweight. By refusing to choose between painter and creature, the work asks whether recognition belongs to appearance, name or licensed intellectual property. Reproduction requests are considered on a case-by-case basis by several legal departments.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/salvador_dolly.png" alt="Salvador Dalí sits inside an elaborate open dollhouse surrounded by miniature rooms, portraits and dreamlike landscapes."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Salvador Dolly</strong>
    <span><em>Dreamhouse with Soft Tenant</em>, 1936</span>
    <span>Oil on panel, hinges, miniature furnishings</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">The open house makes interiority architectural and architecture available for play. Dalí occupies its centre at a scale too large for the rooms yet too settled to leave them, converting domestic space into a theatre of self-possession. Each miniature door promises another unconscious chamber. Most are locked because the Collection misplaced the very small keys.</p>
</figure>

<figure class="collection-work">
  <div class="collection-frame collection-frame--landscape"><img src="/assets/images/artists/claude_monet_laundering.png" alt="Claude Monet feeding euro banknotes and water lilies into a washing machine in a sunlit laundrette."></div>
  <figcaption class="collection-label">
    <strong>Claude Monet</strong>
    <span><em>Laundering</em>, 1916</span>
    <span>Oil on canvas</span>
  </figcaption>
  <p class="collection-wall-text">Light draws money, flowers and machinery into a shared economy of colour. Denominations remain legible at the edges of forms already becoming petals, as value enters a cycle that promises renewal through agitation. An allegorical reading has been proposed. The man may simply have left the money in his trousers. The Collection accepts no responsibility for shrinkage.</p>
</figure>
