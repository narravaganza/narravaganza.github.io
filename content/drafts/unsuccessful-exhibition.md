**From: Mireille, Gallery Morcel**  
**Subject: Re: Exhibition proposal**

Dear Ariel,

Thank you for your proposal. We are still reviewing submissions, and I should emphasise that an invitation to develop a proposal does not constitute a commitment to exhibit.

At present, the work seems to be at an early stage. The visual associations are accessible, although it is difficult to establish from these examples whether they support a sustained enquiry. We would need a clearer sense of how the individual gestures accumulate into a practice.

The washing machines may offer a point of entry into our programme's consideration of domestic labour and the infrastructures of care. The delivery vehicle could extend this towards questions of artistic mobility, provided the relationship is developed beyond the immediate association.

Could you send a statement situating the work in relation to these concerns, alongside a CV and details of previous exhibitions? Two hundred words maximum for the statement.

Also, are these available as files? Our current exhibitor supplied six pieces on a laptop which he takes home whenever he needs it.

Best wishes,
Mireille

**From: Mireille**  
**Subject: Statement, for approval**

Dear Ariel,

With production support confirmed, we can offer you a presentation in the project space. This would be an opportunity to develop the enquiry within a supported framework, with the final selection and interpretation agreed by the gallery.

I've drafted a statement from our exchanges:

> Working across the expanded field of image production, Ariel's practice brings histories of artistic authorship into dialogue with the material and linguistic infrastructures through which everyday life is maintained. Through a sustained attention to the proper name as a site of cultural investment, the works trace the passage between the canonical and the consumable, asking how inherited forms of recognition might be made available to other uses, other bodies, other ways of being together.
>
> The domestic emerges here as a contested space of production in which care, repetition and exhaustion acquire a provisional visibility. Familiar figures circulate through unfamiliar relations of service and exchange, inviting the viewer to negotiate their own implication in the economies that organise the encounter. In holding these relations open, Ariel proposes a practice of attention through which the ordinary might become legible as a field of collective possibility.

Are there other works which might extend this proposition towards questions of nourishment and bodily care? We would need to see these before confirming their inclusion.
